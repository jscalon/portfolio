Complete redesign of the official website of **Botinfy**, a software development company.

## The problem

The previous site was built on the same technology as the new one, so the problem was not
technical but one of **design and impact**: it looked basic and conveyed no
professionalism. For a company whose business is *building software*, the official site is
a critical showcase — a visitor who arrives expecting good work and finds an unfinished
site has their answer before reading a single line.

## The solution

A **seven-page site** — among them Home, About, Products, Careers, Policies and Terms —
with a far stronger visual identity and the experience looked after at the three sizes the
design covers: mobile, tablet and desktop.

Decisions that define the result:

- **Motion with intent.** The animations are built with GSAP: image carousels and light
  effects over headings and buttons. They give the brand presence without getting in the
  way of reading, which is what separates a site with character from a restless one.
- **Mobile is not an inherited adaptation.** The existing design covered desktop only, so
  the mobile version was designed from scratch before being built, rather than letting the
  desktop layout shrink however it could.
- **Images optimized** through Next.js's `Image` component, which serves each device the
  size and format it needs instead of one file for everyone.

## My role

I built the site's frontend and designed the mobile version in full. Development took a
month.

- **Design (UI):** the desktop version started from a base made by the team, which I
  contributed ideas and adjustments to; **the mobile version I designed entirely myself**
  in Figma, because it did not exist. The brand guidelines were already defined and I
  worked within them.
- **Frontend:** Next.js, TypeScript and Tailwind CSS, across three breakpoints — mobile,
  tablet and desktop.
- **Animation:** GSAP for the carousels and the light effects.

## Project status

The site is live at [botinfy.com](https://botinfy.com), though it is no longer exactly the
one I delivered: the company restructured part of the content and the pages describing the
agents it offered are gone. **Home and About remain practically identical to how I built
them**, and are the pages that best represent the work described here.
